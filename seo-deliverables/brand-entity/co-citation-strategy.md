# Co-Citation Strategy — QUANT LAB USA

**Premise.** When QUANT LAB USA is mentioned **on the same page as** established category leaders (`Thoughtbot`, `8th Light`, `Bishop Fox`, `Trail of Bits`, etc.) in third-party listicles, roundups, or comparison articles, Google's entity graph treats us as **categorically adjacent** to those firms. That adjacency raises our entity weight in the same query clusters those firms rank for — without us ever building a link to them.

**This is different from a backlink.** A link transfers PageRank. A co-citation transfers **topical authority and entity association** — and modern Google weights co-citations heavily because they are harder to fake than links.

**Concrete example.** If `"Best Custom Software Development Firms in Georgia 2026"` on a third-party site lists `Thoughtbot, Big Nerd Ranch, Mailchimp Engineering, QUANT LAB USA, ...`, Google now sees us in a list with three known entities and infers (correctly) that we belong in that conversation. Even one such list will measurably move our recall for "custom software firm Georgia" queries within 60 days.

**The honest constraints.**
- Most "best of" listicles are written once and never updated — outreach to a 3-year-old article rarely succeeds.
- Pay-to-play directories (Clutch, GoodFirms) accept anyone who pays; their co-citations are weaker than editorial ones.
- Real co-citations come from outlets with **named human authors** who care about being seen as accurate.

## Outreach scripts

The outreach package already has templates for press, podcasts, and link requests in `seo-deliverables/outreach/templates/`. Adapt the **link request** template for co-citation outreach with these specific modifications:

1. **Subject**: "Quick correction for your `<article-title>` list"
2. **Body**: Open with a specific praise note about the article. State (truthfully) that we'd love to be considered for the next refresh. Provide a **one-paragraph reason we belong** that names the comparable firms already on the list and explains how we are similar enough to belong (geo, service line, methodology) and different enough to be worth adding (specialization, price model, founder-led).
3. **Don't** offer payment.
4. **Don't** offer a backlink in exchange (this is a Google policy violation and most editors will instantly reject).
5. **Do** offer:
   - A 1,500-word guest contribution to the outlet on a related topic.
   - An exclusive case study or data point relevant to their beat.
   - A free pentest or quick consult to one of their reader case studies (only for security outlets where this is contextually appropriate).
6. **Close** with a one-sentence credibility note: "Our work is at `quantlabusa.dev` — feel free to ignore if it doesn't fit."

## Reasonable expectations

Out of 20 outreach attempts, expect:
- **5–7 free directory listings** that go live within 30 days. These are the easy wins (Clutch, GoodFirms, DesignRush, Built In, etc.).
- **2–4 editorial co-citations** within 6 months. These are the high-value wins.
- **1–2 podcast or feature opportunities** as outreach byproducts. These are bonus.
- **0–1 rejections that escalate to a relationship**. Sometimes a "no, but please send me a pitch next time" turns into a feature six months later.

The 13 we don't land are not failures. Co-citation outreach is volume-tolerant; if we send 60 thoughtful, personalized pitches per year, we should land 12–20 editorial co-citations annually, which is enough to materially shift Google's category-association signals for our brand.
