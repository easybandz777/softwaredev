Hi A List Apart editorial team,

A List Apart's recent essay on accessibility maturity models was the rare piece that distinguished "WCAG-conformant" from "actually accessible to the human." I want to extend that distinction with a customer base most accessibility writing skips: the 55-year-old contractor on a phone, in bright sun, holding a piece of paper.

I am Bill Beltz, founder of QUANT LAB USA - a Macon, GA custom software firm. We build for blue-collar trades businesses across the Southeast. The customers buying our clients' services are not 28-year-old urbanites; they are homeowners in their 50s and 60s, contractors in their 40s and 50s, marina-dock customers, towing dispatchers.

The A List Apart essay I want to write:

**Working title:** "WCAG 2.2 for the customer your design system forgot"

**Premise:** Most accessibility writing treats the disabled user as a category - VoiceOver-on-iPhone, JAWS-on-Windows. The harder accessibility category is the user who is not disabled but is constrained: bright sun, gloves, reading glasses, 1-handed phone use, a 4G connection in a truck. WCAG 2.2 covers these contexts. Most teams skip them because the testing burden looks high.

**Specifics:**
- The Northcrest Fence & Gate estimate flow - mobile-first, has to work in a driveway in Alpharetta; what changed when we audited the form for 1-handed-with-glove use (input sizes went up, hit targets went to 48px, error states moved above-the-line)
- The Coastal Yacht Services deposit flow - marina-dockside customer with patchy 4G; what changed when we mandated a 3G performance budget (LCP under 2.0s on 3G, eliminate font-loading FOUT)
- The Bridgepointe Painting estimate request flow - audited for outdoor visibility, contrast ratios pushed to 7:1, sticky-keyboard avoidance with viewport meta refinements
- The four "we used to do this; now we do that" patterns we adopted

**Spec:** 1,800-2,400 words, code samples, before/after screenshots from real production deploys, all anonymized where customer-facing. Original to A List Apart.
