# Follow-up Sequences - 3-Touch and 5-Touch Cadences

The single biggest leverage in cold outreach is the follow-up. Cold-pitch hit rates on the first email are 3-10%. After 3-4 follow-ups, hit rates climb to 20-30%. Most people stop at one. Do not.

This file specifies two cadences:
- **3-touch** - for most pitches (guest posts, roundup inclusions, podcast pitches)
- **5-touch** - for high-value sales/prospect outreach (LinkedIn DM sequences) - already documented in `linkedin-dms.md`; this file recaps for completeness

---

# 3-Touch Cadence

**Use for:** Guest post pitches, podcast pitches, roundup inclusions, resource page suggestions, broken-link replacements, press pitches, expert-quote source applications

**Touch schedule:**
- Day 0: Initial pitch
- Day +5: Follow-up #1 (gentle bump)
- Day +14: Follow-up #2 (final, asks for redirect)
- Day +14: STOP. Revisit in 90 days only with a new angle.

---

## Day 0 - Initial pitch

Use the appropriate template from `templates/`. Send Tuesday-Thursday 9-11 AM ET.

---

## Day +5 - Follow-up #1 (gentle bump)

**Subject:** Re: [original subject - keep the threading]

---

Hi [First Name],

Bumping this in case it got buried. No pressure if it is not a fit - I just wanted to confirm it arrived.

Bill

---

Rules:
- Send AS A REPLY to the original email (keeps threading intact for inbox sorting)
- Under 30 words
- Never re-pitch the entire idea
- Never apologize for following up
- Never use "I haven't heard back" or "you didn't respond" phrasing

---

## Day +14 - Follow-up #2 (final, asks for redirect)

**Subject:** Last note - [topic]

---

Hi [First Name],

Final follow-up from me. If [opportunity / pitch / inclusion request] is not the right fit, no problem at all - I will close the loop on my side.

If there is a better contact for [publication / page / outlet / podcast] outreach, I would appreciate the redirect. Otherwise I will check back next quarter with a different angle.

Thanks either way.

Bill Beltz
QUANT LAB USA - (770) 652-1282

---

Rules:
- Acknowledge this is the final touch
- Offer the recipient an easy out ("no problem at all")
- Ask for redirect to the right contact - this often gets a response when the original recipient does not have time
- Promise to revisit in 90 days with a new angle (and actually do it)

---

## After Day +14 - Stop

Do NOT pursue further. Two follow-ups is the polite ceiling. Three+ feels like harassment and damages the relationship for a future angle.

Revisit in 90 days with:
- A different topic
- A different proof point (new client, new OSS release, new published work)
- A different subject line variant
- A different contact at the same publication if available

# 5-Touch Cadence (LinkedIn DM sequences)

**Use for:** Sales prospecting via LinkedIn DM (already documented in `linkedin-dms.md`)

Touch schedule:
- Day 0: Connection request (under 300 chars)
- Day +1 after accept: Follow-up #1 (introduction + soft pain hook)
- Day +4 after F1: Follow-up #2 (direct question)
- Day +8 after F2: Follow-up #3 (offers free resource)
- Day +21 after F3: Follow-up #4 (case study / new angle)
- Day +45 after F4: Final touch (asks for a 12-month pause)

This sequence is fully drafted in `linkedin-dms.md` for 6 ICPs. Refer there.

---

# When to deviate from these cadences

## Deviation #1 - Editor asks "send more detail"

If the editor or host replies with a question (e.g., "what would the article look like?"), respond within 4 hours with detailed answer. Then the cadence resets - their next touch is on them, not you.

If they go silent after asking for detail and you have responded:
- Day +5: Follow-up #1 (one line: "Bumping in case my detail got buried")
- Day +14: Follow-up #2 (final)

## Deviation #2 - High-value target (DR 90+)

For Wired, TechCrunch, GitHub Blog, Stack Overflow Blog: the 3-touch cadence stays the same but the follow-up timing extends:
- Day 0: Initial
- Day +14: Follow-up #1 (these editors are slower)
- Day +45: Follow-up #2 (final)

## Deviation #3 - Inbound interest

If the target initiates contact (responds to a HARO query, requests a quote, accepts a podcast pitch): no follow-up cadence applies. They are driving. Match their pace.

## Deviation #4 - Time-sensitive pitch

If pitching a news hook (e.g., "We just launched J5 Sales OS today"): the follow-up cadence compresses:
- Day 0: Initial
- Day +2: Follow-up #1
- Day +5: Follow-up #2 (final - the news has aged)

# Anti-patterns to avoid

- **The over-eager follow-up:** Day +1 follow-up looks desperate. Wait at least 3 days for first follow-up.
- **The novel follow-up:** Writing a longer follow-up than the original pitch. Follow-ups are short. Always.
- **The guilt-trip follow-up:** "I haven't heard from you" / "I'm a bit disappointed" - never. Recipients owe you nothing.
- **The bait-and-switch follow-up:** Pretending you're sending something new when you're following up. Always reply-to-thread for follow-ups #1.
- **The five-follow-up:** Three is the max for cold pitches. Four+ is harassment.
- **The "did you get my email" follow-up:** Of course they got it. They saw it and decided not to respond. Don't ask.

# Conversion math (why follow-ups matter)

Sample math from 100 cold pitches:
- After initial pitch (Day 0): 8 replies, 3 acceptances
- After follow-up #1 (Day +5): +5 replies, +2 acceptances (total: 13 / 5)
- After follow-up #2 (Day +14): +3 replies, +1 acceptance (total: 16 / 6)

Going from 1 to 3 touches doubles your acceptance rate. The marginal cost per follow-up is 2-3 minutes. The follow-up is the highest-ROI activity in the campaign.
