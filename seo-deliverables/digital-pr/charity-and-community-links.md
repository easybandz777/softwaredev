# Charity & Community Links — Genuine Support for Organic Backlinks

**Purpose:** A list of Georgia nonprofits, community groups, and open-source projects QUANT LAB USA INC can genuinely support — and earn organic .org/.edu backlinks from as a downstream benefit.

**Hard rule (same as scholarship strategy):** The support has to be real. Token sponsorships chosen exclusively for a backlink are detectable by both humans and Google, harm the brand, and produce links that get devalued. The list below pairs each opportunity with a real reason QUANT LAB should support it — beyond the link value — so each support decision is defensible on its merits.

**The default support model:**
- **Cash sponsorship:** Small ($100-$500) annual sponsorship for organizations that publish sponsor lists
- **Pro-bono technical work:** 10-40 hours/year of donated technical work for organizations whose mission genuinely fits
- **Open-source contributions:** Substantive PRs and bug reports to tools the firm actually uses

**Link value framing:**
- A .org link from a well-maintained nonprofit (Habitat for Humanity, United Way, food bank network) is high authority — often DA 50-75
- A sponsor-list link from a community group is moderate authority but locally hyper-relevant
- An open-source contributor credit link from a tool's README or contributor page produces a link from the project's main domain
- A "client" or "supporter" testimonial on a nonprofit's website produces a high-quality, hand-built editorial link

## How to choose which 5 to commit to in year 1

Pick organizations using these filters, in order:

1. **Geographic relevance:** Is QUANT LAB physically present in this organization's community? (Macon > Atlanta > rest of Georgia > national)
2. **Mission alignment:** Does the org's mission overlap with what the company actually believes in? Tech education for underserved youth = high alignment. A car-show sponsorship = low alignment.
3. **Practical fit:** Can the founder actually attend events, volunteer, or contribute substantively?
4. **Cost / benefit:** Is the dollar cost sustainable AT QUANT LAB's current revenue level?
5. **Backlink benefit:** Last filter, not first.

Year 1 recommended commitments (5):

1. **Macon Volunteer Clinic** — $250 sponsor + ~20 hours pro-bono tech work
2. **Mercer University CCJ** — $500 sponsor + 1 guest lecture
3. **TechBridge (Atlanta)** — Pro-bono project for one matched nonprofit (~30 hours)
4. **Greater Macon Chamber of Commerce** — $400 annual membership
5. **OWASP** — substantive open-source contribution (no cash)

Year 2 adds 2-3 more from Tier 1 / Tier 2 based on year-1 experience and relationship traction.

## Anti-patterns

**Things NOT to do:**

- Donate $20 to a charity and ask for a sponsor link. Charities can detect this and so can Google. The sponsorship has to be meaningful at the org's scale (a $20 donation to a $5M-revenue nonprofit is invisible; a $250 donation to a Macon clinic with $400K of programming is meaningful).
- Sponsor an org whose mission is irrelevant to the business or founder solely for the link.
- Hire someone offshore to "ghost contribute" to open-source repos. The credit goes to the actual author, not the firm; and the practice gets detected.
- "Sponsor" a sham nonprofit that exists primarily to sell sponsorship slots. These exist (the same way sham scholarship programs do); they look like real nonprofits at a glance but have no real programming.
- Demand a do-follow link as a sponsorship condition. Most legitimate nonprofits will give do-follow links if asked nicely, but demanding it changes the relationship into a transactional one and is often refused.
- Donate to a national org with no local connection just because it's high DA. The link still works but the mismatch is detectable.
