## 1. Strategic premise

Domain authority is the single biggest constraint on QUANT LAB USA's organic traction. The site already has solid foundations — full schema, 100+ indexed pages, 5 published press releases, distribution playbook, a /press and /press-kit setup. What it does not yet have is **earned editorial links from high-authority publications**. Those are the link type Google weighs most heavily, and they compound for years.

Digital PR — as opposed to paid placements, link schemes, or generic "PR distribution" — is the discipline of giving real journalists something genuinely worth publishing, then making it trivially easy for them to do so. The 6-month plan below is designed for a one-person shop with a single founder-spokesperson, a real services business behind it, and a budget closer to $0–$3,000 than $30,000 per campaign.

**Realistic success criteria (6 months, not 6 weeks):**

- **3–6 do-follow editorial backlinks** from DA-50+ publications by month 6 (Atlanta Business Chronicle, Hypepotamus, Built In, CIO Dive, Dark Reading, Inc.com contributor pieces, Fast Company contributor pieces, regional Georgia outlets, mid-tier trade pubs).
- **8–15 podcast guest appearances** by month 6 (most pods publish show-notes pages with do-follow links to the guest's site).
- **15–30 expert-commentary citations** by month 6 (Qwoted / Source of Sources / Help A B2B Writer style — each citation produces a do-follow link and a quotable mention).
- **1–2 award wins or finalist mentions** (Clutch, GoodFirms, regional Atlanta Business Chronicle, Built In Best Places — finalist status alone produces a link).
- **1 scholarship .edu page launched** with multi-year compound link from at least 1 .edu source.
- **5+ charity/community .org links** earned through genuine open-source contributions, sponsorships, or pro-bono work.
- **Aggregate Domain Authority delta:** +5 to +12 DA points over the 6 months (Moz scale), measured as the difference between the May 2026 baseline (~10–18 estimated) and the late-November 2026 reading.

The campaign is deliberately slow-burn. Digital PR earned correctly compounds — every editorial link added to the property continues paying rent in Google rankings for years. Every paid "guaranteed placement" service that has ever existed (link networks, sponsored guest posts on PBN sites, "DA 90 dofollow" Fiverr offers) is the opposite: the link goes live, then either gets devalued or actively penalized within 6–24 months. The 6-month plan is built around **only the earned kind**, with zero paid placement and zero link-buying.

## 2. Quarterly themes

The two halves of the plan use two different angle anchors. Each quarter has a "campaign hero" — the single anchor story journalists are pitched on — plus a small portfolio of supporting reactive pitches and expert-commentary plays that run in the background regardless of theme.

### Q1: June 2026 — August 2026 — **"The Real Cost of Custom Software in 2026"**

**Hero anchor:** An updated, expanded 2026 Custom Software Cost Index. The existing `/2026-software-development-cost-guide` is the foundation — Q1 turns it into a journalist-grade research piece with original geographic data, methodology notes, and reusable charts.

**Why journalists care:** Cost stories are evergreen. "What does an MVP actually cost in [year]?" is a question every small-business writer at Inc, Fast Company, CIO Dive, Built In, and every regional outlet gets asked annually by their audience. A vendor that publishes defensible, citation-ready numbers becomes the source for the year.

**Supporting plays:**
- Data study: "Software Development Cost Index by US City — 2026 Edition" (see `data-study-ideas.md` #1).
- Expert commentary bank pulls on "MVP cost estimation," "build vs buy thresholds," and "offshore vs US developer rate gaps."
- Founder podcast tour pitch angle: "Why most MVP estimates are wrong by 2-3x — and the line items that get missed."

**Target outlets:** Inc.com, Fast Company, CIO Dive, Built In, Hypepotamus, Atlanta Inno, Atlanta Business Chronicle, GeorgiaTrend, regional small-business publications.

**Quarterly cadence:**
- **June, week 1:** Publish the Cost Index research piece. Submit to GlobeNewswire + free Tier 2 wires. Send to 20 hand-curated journalists.
- **June, weeks 2–4:** Follow-up cycle. React to inbound. Repurpose into 4 LinkedIn essays.
- **July, weeks 1–2:** Pitch contributor angles to Inc.com, Fast Company, CIO Dive based on Cost Index findings.
- **July–August:** Pitch 6 podcast guest appearances on the cost-of-MVP angle. Submit Cost Index to industry awards (Clutch, GoodFirms).
- **August, week 4:** Compile Q1 coverage report. Identify what worked.

### Q2: September 2026 — November 2026 — **"AI-Native Development vs Offshore — the New Geography of Software Work"**

**Hero anchor:** Original survey + qualitative interviews on whether AI-assisted US-based development is undercutting offshore agency pricing. This is the controversial-but-defensible take. The data exists in the firm's own engagement records and can be supplemented with a public-facing 50–200 respondent survey of small-business operators who have used both offshore and US dev shops.

**Why journalists care:** This is a *story* — not just a list. It cuts across AI, labor markets, immigration, regional tech economies, and small-business cost structures. It has natural pickup angles for tech press (TechCrunch, VentureBeat, The Information), business press (Inc, Fast Company, Bloomberg Industry Group), policy press (Axios, Politico), and regional press (Atlanta Business Chronicle, Hypepotamus, every "tech hub" beat).

**Supporting plays:**
- Data study: "How AI-Assisted Dev Changed Cost Per Feature: 2024 vs 2026" (see `data-study-ideas.md` #2).
- Data study: "Time-to-MVP — Quoted vs Actual, 100 Engagements" (see `data-study-ideas.md` #4).
- Expert commentary on "AI replacing junior devs," "offshore vs domestic dev quality," "the AI productivity wedge."
- Founder podcast tour pitch angle: "What 100 AI-built features looks like in production."
- Public scholarship launch (see `scholarship-link-strategy.md`) tied to the theme: "US-based AI-native developer scholarship."

**Target outlets:** TechCrunch, VentureBeat, The Information, Fast Company, Inc.com, CIO Dive, Built In, Pragmatic Engineer newsletter, Lenny's Newsletter, Hypepotamus, Atlanta Inno.

**Quarterly cadence:**
- **September, week 1:** Survey opens. Publicized through LinkedIn, X, newsletter, Indie Hackers, r/Entrepreneur.
- **September, weeks 2–4:** Survey collection + qualitative interviews with 8–12 client and prospect operators.
- **October, week 1:** Research piece publishes. Embargo to top 6 outlets (3-day lead).
- **October, week 2:** Wire + free aggregator push. Personal pitches to 25 journalists.
- **October, weeks 3–4:** Follow-up cycle. Contributor pitches to Inc, Fast Company.
- **November, weeks 1–2:** Award applications (Inc 5000 if eligible, Clutch annual, Built In Best Places).
- **November, weeks 3–4:** Scholarship launches publicly. .edu outreach begins.
- **November, week 4:** Q2 coverage report + 6-month review.

## 4. Reactive — earned-media triage

Three classes of news triggers reactive pitching. Bill should keep a Google Alert for each. When one fires:

1. **AI / developer-tools news** (e.g., Anthropic release, OpenAI release, Cursor/Replit/Bolt funding, a notable AI-built product going viral). Within 90 minutes, draft a 2-sentence reaction quote tying it to the cost guide, the engagement experience, or the survey data. Fire to 5–10 Qwoted/SOS sources that historically cover the topic.

2. **Stripe / payments news** (new Stripe feature, Square outage, Adyen pricing change). The firm has deep production Stripe Connect experience; this is a natural authority position. React within 2 hours.

3. **Penetration testing / cyber-incident news** (a notable breach, especially one where the attack path matches public attack-chain documentation). React with a credentialed quote on what likely happened, framed through MITRE ATT&CK, not vendor-speak.

**Speed beats elegance.** A pitched quote 90 minutes after the news drops will get used. The same quote 24 hours later will not.

## 5. Founder positioning ladder

Each quarter, the founder's public byline footprint should expand by one rung. The ladder, in order:

1. **Quoted source in someone else's article** (already happening — keep accelerating).
2. **Contributor byline at Inc.com or Fast Company** (free contributor programs; pitch a single column).
3. **Bylined opinion piece at TechCrunch, VentureBeat, or CIO Dive** (harder; requires a clear thesis that is not promotional).
4. **Keynote or panel slot at a regional tech event** (Render Atlanta, Atlanta Tech Village events, Mercer/Georgia Tech sponsored panels).
5. **Newsletter mention in The Pragmatic Engineer, Lenny's Newsletter, or Stratechery** (one of these per year is realistic if the work warrants it).

Move one rung up per quarter. Do not try to skip rungs.

## 9. Anti-patterns — what NOT to do

These are practices that look like PR but harm the long-term link profile. None of them appear in this plan.

- **Paid guest-post networks** (Outreach.io, generic "we'll publish your article on 1,000 sites" services). Google has been devaluing these since at least 2017. Almost all such links are either no-followed, deindexed, or in a manual-action queue.
- **PBNs (private blog networks)**. Same problem, plus active penalty risk.
- **HARO/Qwoted answers that are pure marketing** without a real expert insight. Editors increasingly reject these and some platforms ban repeat offenders.
- **Paying for inclusion in "Top 10 Best [X] Companies" listicles** unless the publisher is a genuinely editorial outlet (Clutch, GoodFirms, G2 — these are accepted; "BestRatedAgencies.io" type sites — not accepted).
- **Reciprocal link exchanges** ("I'll link to your site if you link to mine"). Google has been able to detect these patterns since the early 2010s.
- **Auto-spinning press releases** for SEO purposes. Modern aggregators no-follow most of these links, and the residual brand-mention value is negative when the copy reads as machine-generated.
- **Fake awards** (paid placement on award lists that exist only to sell paid placements). See `awards-applications.md` for the curated list of *real* awards, and which ones to ignore.
