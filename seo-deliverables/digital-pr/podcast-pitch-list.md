# Podcast Pitch List — 25 Shows for Founder Guest Appearances

**Purpose:** Curated list of 25 podcasts where Bill Beltz (founder, QUANT LAB USA INC) is a credible, on-topic guest. Podcast guesting is one of the highest-leverage digital-PR plays: each appearance produces show-notes pages with do-follow links to the guest's site, plus an audio asset that lives indefinitely.

**Why podcasting is good for QUANT LAB:**
- Show-notes pages produce do-follow backlinks from the podcast's hosting domain (typically the podcast's homepage DA + a permanent episode-level URL).
- Audio content is hard to game with AI/spam, so podcasts are weighted higher than blog comments or directory submissions.
- A 45-minute conversation produces dozens of quotable segments that can be repurposed into LinkedIn clips, X posts, blog excerpts.
- Repeat guesting builds a media flywheel — once you're on 5 podcasts, hosts of other shows start finding you.

**Founder's four prepared topic angles:**

1. **"AI-native development methodology — what an AI-built feature actually looks like in production"** — for engineering-focused shows (Pragmatic Engineer, Software Engineering Daily, indie hacker shows).
2. **"US-based vs offshore software development in 2026 — the new TCO math"** — for business shows, bootstrap shows, startup shows.
3. **"Scaling a one-person consultancy without becoming an agency"** — for solo/bootstrap-founder shows.
4. **"Building software inside the Macon / Georgia tech ecosystem"** — for regional, civic, and entrepreneurship shows.

**Pitch principles:**
- Listen to at least 2 recent episodes before pitching. Reference one in the pitch.
- Lead with a specific topic angle, not a bio. Hosts care about what you'll say, not who you are.
- Offer 2-3 angle options so the host can pick what fits their show.
- Keep pitch email to under 200 words.
- Send only 1 podcast pitch per week per host (do not blast).
- Provide an existing audio sample (a previous podcast appearance, a recorded talk, or a 60-second voice memo answering one of your angles) — this dramatically increases acceptance rate.

**Tier structure:** Tier 1 = aspirational national shows (low probability per pitch but high payoff). Tier 2 = realistic mid-tier shows (higher probability). Tier 3 = niche / regional shows (highest probability, smaller reach per show but useful for early flywheel).

## Pitching workflow

| Step | Time per podcast |
|---|---|
| Listen to 2 recent episodes | 90 min |
| Draft custom pitch | 15 min |
| Send pitch | 5 min |
| Follow up after 7 days if no response | 5 min |
| Pre-record (book + prep + record) | 90 min |
| Post-promote (LinkedIn, X, newsletter, /press page update) | 30 min |
| **Total per booked appearance** | **~4 hours** |

At 1-2 bookings/month, podcasting is a 4–8 hour/month investment that produces 1-2 permanent do-follow backlinks each plus the audio asset.

**Booking conversion rate:** Realistic expectation is 10–20% — 6 pitches/week × 4 weeks = 24 pitches/month → 2–5 bookings/month.

---

## Repurposing every appearance

Every booked podcast produces 7 derivative assets:

1. **Show-notes page link** to quantlabusa.dev (the primary SEO payoff)
2. **Embed of the episode** on the QUANT LAB `/press` page in a "Recent appearances" section
3. **LinkedIn post** linking the episode with 2-3 quote callouts
4. **X thread** (5–8 tweets) with audio clips and key quotes
5. **Newsletter mention** in the next QUANT LAB issue
6. **Blog post** expanding on one specific take from the episode (~600 words, links back to episode)
7. **Press kit asset** — add "Recent podcasts" to the press kit downloads page

**Compounding effect:** by month 6, with 8–15 podcast appearances each repurposed 7 ways, the on-site catalog of episode references becomes substantial — a "podcast circuit" credibility signal for both journalists and prospects landing on the press page.
