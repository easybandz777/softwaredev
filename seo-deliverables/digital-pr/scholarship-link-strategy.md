# Scholarship Link Strategy — The Bill Beltz / QUANT LAB USA Software & Cybersecurity Scholarship

**Purpose:** A legitimate scholarship program designed to support Georgia students pursuing software development or cybersecurity careers — and earn high-authority .edu inbound links as a downstream benefit.

**Hard rule:** This is not a sham scholarship. Sham scholarships (the "submit an essay, we'll pick a winner, here's $500" links-only schemes) have been actively penalized by Google since 2017, and several universities now publicly refuse to list scholarships that don't pay out or that lack genuine selection criteria. The program described below is a real, funded, judged program that benefits a real student. The .edu links are a downstream benefit, not the purpose.

---

## 1. Program design

### Name
**The Quant Lab USA Software & Cybersecurity Scholarship**

### Mission
To support one Georgia-resident undergraduate or graduate student pursuing a career in software development, cybersecurity, or applied AI engineering, with a one-time $1,500 award and a 30-minute mentoring session with the founder of QUANT LAB USA INC.

### Award amount
**$1,500** — paid as a single check to the recipient's educational institution (financial aid office) to be applied toward tuition, books, or related educational expenses.

The $1,500 number is deliberately chosen:
- Substantial enough to be meaningful to a student
- Within range a one-founder firm can sustainably commit to annually
- Above the IRS reporting threshold ($600) — this means there is a real 1099 process, real paperwork, real legitimacy
- Below the federal financial aid impact threshold for most students at most institutions, so it doesn't reduce the student's other aid

### Frequency
**One annual award.** Application window opens September 1, closes March 1, winner announced May 1, funds disbursed by June 30 of the same year.

### Eligibility criteria

To qualify, an applicant must:

1. Be a current undergraduate or graduate student at any accredited Georgia college, university, or technical institute (the list includes Mercer University, Georgia State, Georgia Tech, University of Georgia, Kennesaw State, Georgia Southern, Middle Georgia State, Wesleyan College, Mercer's College of Continuing and Professional Studies, Augusta University, Albany State, Savannah State, Fort Valley State, Valdosta State, Columbus State, Atlanta Metropolitan State, Clayton State, Atlanta Technical College, Central Georgia Technical College, and all other accredited public and private institutions in Georgia).
2. Be enrolled in a degree, certificate, or formal credential program in computer science, software engineering, cybersecurity, information systems, applied mathematics, or a closely related field. Self-taught and bootcamp students are not eligible — this is a college/university scholarship.
3. Have a minimum 2.8 cumulative GPA on a 4.0 scale.
4. Be a US citizen or permanent resident.
5. Be a Georgia resident at the time of application.

### Application materials

The applicant submits:

1. **Personal statement (500-800 words):** Why they're pursuing this field and what they hope to contribute. Honest answers are weighted more than aspirational ones.

2. **Technical artifact:** One of:
   - A link to a public GitHub repository they personally authored substantially
   - A link to a published technical writeup, blog post, or paper they personally wrote
   - A link to a CTF (capture the flag) writeup or security disclosure they participated in
   - A link to an open-source contribution where they are listed as a contributor

3. **Transcript:** Official or unofficial transcript showing current academic standing.

4. **Resume:** One page.

5. **One reference:** Email contact for one professor, mentor, or employer who can vouch for the applicant's character. We email the reference; the reference is not required to write a letter.

### Selection criteria

The judging panel (the founder + one external mentor — likely a Mercer CS faculty member, recruited via the scholarship's launch outreach) weighs:

- **Demonstrated capability (40%)** — evidence in the technical artifact that the applicant can actually do the work
- **Articulated motivation (30%)** — the personal statement is read for clarity of thought, not for inspirational tone
- **Academic standing (15%)** — GPA, course rigor, progress toward degree
- **Reference confirmation (15%)** — the reference's response

Selection is single-blind: the judging panel does not see applicant names until after scoring.

### What the recipient receives

1. **$1,500 disbursed to their institution's financial aid office** for application toward educational expenses.
2. **A 30-minute video or in-person mentoring session** with Bill Beltz, founder.
3. **A LinkedIn recommendation** post-graduation if requested.
4. **Optional**: invitation to interview for a paid internship at QUANT LAB USA INC (no guarantee; merit-based; subject to availability).

### What the recipient does NOT have to do

- No essay marketing copy mentioning the company
- No requirement to link to or follow QUANT LAB USA INC on any platform
- No requirement to give a testimonial
- No requirement to use any specific technology
- No requirement to disclose application materials publicly

This is critical. Linking the scholarship's continued payment to promotional activity would invalidate the program both legally (potential tax issues) and ethically (turns the "scholarship" into a marketing contract). Recipients receive the scholarship because they earned it; what they do afterward is theirs.

## 4. Cost / benefit analysis

### Direct costs (annual)

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Scholarship award (1 × $1,500) | $1,500 |
| Application form (Tally Pro) | $0-300/year |
| Founder time: program setup year 1 | ~20 hours @ self-cost |
| Founder time: judging panel work annually | ~10 hours |
| Founder time: outreach to financial aid offices | ~12 hours year 1; ~4 hours per year ongoing |
| Mentoring session with recipient | ~30 min |
| **Year 1 total** | **~$1,500 cash + ~45 hours of founder time** |
| **Year 2+ ongoing** | **~$1,500 cash + ~15 hours of founder time** |

### Expected backlink return

Realistic forecast for year 1:

- **.edu links from Georgia institutions:** 6-12 (assuming 25-40% acceptance rate across 20 Tier 1 institutions)
- **.com aggregator links:** 3-5 from Fastweb / Scholarships.com / Niche / BigFuture / Cappex
- **.org links:** 1-2 from specialty professional society scholarship lists

**Total: ~10-19 inbound links per year.**

The .edu links are the asymmetric value. A single do-follow .edu link from a DA 80+ institution (Georgia Tech is DA 92, UGA is DA 90, Mercer is DA 75) is conservatively worth several thousand dollars in equivalent paid SEO value — and that's per link, per year, compounding. The full backlink portfolio from year 1 is conservatively worth $15,000-40,000 in equivalent paid SEO impact, against a $1,500 cash outlay and a real student receiving a real award.

### Compounding effect

Years 2-5 produce additional backlinks each year as financial aid lists get refreshed, new institutions are added to outreach, and past-recipient announcements drive additional press mentions. The backlinks from year 1 typically remain live for the lifetime of the financial aid office's scholarship database.

## 5. Anti-patterns — what makes this NOT a sham scholarship

For comparison, here is what the typical "scholarship link scheme" looks like and how this program differs:

| Sham scholarship pattern | This program |
|---|---|
| $500-1,000 award; minimal eligibility | $1,500 award; real eligibility |
| "Essay about [our industry]" — marketing content | Personal statement on student's career goals |
| No real judging; first 10 applicants win | Two-judge panel; criteria published |
| Winner does not exist publicly | Winner is announced publicly (with consent) |
| Disbursed via PayPal/Venmo | Disbursed via 1099 to recipient's institution |
| Backlink instructions to recipient | No promotional requirement on recipient |
| One-time, never repeated | Annually recurring, multi-year commitment |
| No mentor / follow-up | 30-min mentoring session + LinkedIn recommendation |
| Email outreach blasted to all .edu | Curated, personalized outreach to Georgia institutions first |

Google has been able to detect the sham pattern since at least 2017 and many universities have stopped accepting scholarship listings without proof of past disbursement. Sticking to the legitimate pattern is the only sustainable approach.

## 7. Legal and tax considerations

The scholarship is structured to comply with IRS rules on scholarships and educational assistance:

- The award is paid to the institution's financial aid office, not directly to the student, to maximize the chance the funds count as qualified educational expenses (tuition, fees, required books) under IRS rules.
- The IRS treats the portion of a scholarship used for qualified educational expenses as tax-free for the recipient; the portion used for non-qualified expenses (room and board, optional supplies) is taxable to the recipient.
- QUANT LAB USA INC is the grantor; the scholarship is a business expense (specifically, an educational-grant promotional expense) — consult with the company's CPA to confirm proper accounting treatment.
- Issue a 1099-MISC to the recipient if the unrestricted portion of the award exceeds $600.
- Maintain a written scholarship policy (this document) on file.
- Do not condition award on the recipient's future employment with the company (this avoids triggering employment-tax obligations).

Confirm all of the above with a CPA and, if structured as a private-foundation-style grant program, with an attorney familiar with private-foundation grant rules. The $1,500 / annual / one-recipient structure described here is small enough that ordinary corporate-promotional-grant treatment applies for most companies, but every situation is fact-specific.

## 10. Year 1 commitment

By launching this scholarship, QUANT LAB USA INC commits to:

- Awarding the full $1,500 to a real recipient by June 30, 2027, regardless of how many applications are received
- Running the program for at least 3 consecutive years (year-1-only "test" scholarships do not earn the multi-year .edu listings that produce most of the value)
- Maintaining the scholarship application materials publicly and accurately
- Responding to every applicant who completes the application (acceptance or polite decline)
- Maintaining transparency about the program through public year-end recaps

These commitments are what make the program legitimate and what makes the resulting .edu links sustainable for the long term. A 1-year scholarship is a marketing stunt. A 3+ year scholarship is an institution. Plan and budget for 3+ years from day one.
